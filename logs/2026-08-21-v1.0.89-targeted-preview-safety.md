# v1.0.89 — Targeted Preview Safety

Date: 2026-08-21

## Trigger

The first real-video validation batch after v1.0.88 showed two release blockers remained visible in the exact same seven-scene fixtures:

1. Light scene3 still produced a brown/yellow rectangular-diamond reconstruction patch where the watermark overlaps a strong beige/purple scene boundary.
2. Dark scene5 still collapsed to an implausibly dark brown/black watermark-shaped area even though v1.0.87 had reduced literal zero-clamp failures.

The other dark structured scenes remained broadly good and therefore stay preservation guards. Light scenes1/2/4/7 still retain smaller residual geometry, but they are lower priority than the two destructive failures above.

## Root-cause refinement

### Light scene3

v1.0.88 protected the later structured-consensus stage. The unchanged real preview proved the destructive color borrowing happens earlier. The earlier `applyPaddedTextureRepair()` stage used directional clean anchors without scene-edge protection, so a watermark crossing a real high-contrast boundary could still mix anchors from opposite materials/colors before downstream guards ran.

### Dark scene5

The v1.0.87 adaptive cap was tuned mainly to prevent literal black underflow. The real scene5 preview now shows a broader near-black/brown collapse: destructive, but often above the old 12-luma threshold. The secondary guard likewise only considered pixels at or below 12 luma. This left a gap where the result was visibly wrong yet numerically not black enough to trigger recovery.

## v1.0.89 changes

### Scene-edge-protected padded texture repair

`src/video/textureRepair.js` now:

- measures whole-footprint crossing-scene-edge risk before padded texture reconstruction;
- strongly attenuates padded repair when a protected real boundary crosses the watermark footprint;
- calls `sceneEdgeProtectionAt()` per candidate pixel;
- skips strong real scene-edge pixels entirely;
- continuously reduces blend near weaker real edges;
- exposes `paddedTextureRepair.crossingRisk` and `globalSceneFactor` diagnostics.

This acts before the v1.0.88 structured-consensus protection, closing the earlier unsafe path rather than increasing later cleanup strength.

### Stronger bounded dark underflow prevention

`src/video/adaptiveAlphaUnderflowCap.js` now uses a more conservative local floor for medium/high-alpha dark pixels:

- local-floor percentile: 0.30 -> 0.35;
- floor scale: 0.72 -> 0.88;
- minimum floor: 4 -> 6;
- diagnostic black/near-black threshold: 12 -> 22;
- maximum bounded alpha reduction fraction: 0.42 -> 0.58.

The cap still only reduces effective alpha when the requested inverse would fall below a clean-neighborhood-derived floor.

### Near-black/brown secondary recovery

`src/video/darkUnderflowGuard.js` now recognizes implausible near-black/brown collapse, not only literal black:

- underflow luma ceiling: 12 -> 24;
- collapse floor: 8 -> 6;
- minimum composite lift: 10 -> 8;
- minimum density: 0.025 -> 0.018;
- minimum pixels: 8 -> 6;
- recovery acceptance remains try/measure/accept with scene-edge blocking.

Genuine near-black material is still protected because recovery requires the clean-anchor prediction and the observed composite to both show meaningful evidence that the restored output collapsed below the local scene.

## Regression coverage

Added `tests/v1.0.89-previewSafety.test.mjs` covering:

1. a high-contrast beige/purple scene boundary crossing the watermark, requiring padded repair attenuation and retained boundary contrast;
2. a brown near-black collapse that must be detected and improved even though pixels are above the old 12-luma threshold;
3. genuine near-black material that must remain unchanged.

## Safety principle

No global structured-ring, shape-ghost, protected-rescue, local-tone, smooth-background, empty-zone, or alpha-removal strength was increased. The light-scene change removes an unsafe early reconstruction path. The dark-scene change only widens the mathematical underflow safety envelope and keeps scene-edge guards plus acceptance rollback.
